# 0011. Lazy-load the globe and self-host its textures

Status: accepted
Date: 2026-09-22
Provenance: autonomous

## Context

The globe on `/travel` (react-globe.gl, three.js, h3-js) made up most of a
single 2.4 MB (699 kB gzip) bundle that every route downloaded. Its textures
came from `unpkg.com/three-globe/example/img/...` with no version pinned:
2.4 MB of JPG and PNG, fetched through a redirect from a third-party CDN.

## Decision

Load the globe component with `React.lazy` inside the Travel page over lazy
loading the whole Travel route, because the page title and intro text then
render without waiting for three.js.

Use an empty `Suspense` fallback over a spinner, because the globe's wrapper
box keeps its fixed height and the globe animates in on its own.

Bundle WebP copies of the textures in `src/assets/images/globe/`, converted
from the `three-globe` package in `node_modules` and served with hashed file
names, over fetching them from unpkg. The site then stops depending on
unpkg's uptime and on an unpinned package path. The earth is at quality 70
and the night sky at quality 60, for 0.8 MB in total.

Guard the split with a Playwright smoke check that caps the site's own
scripts on `/` at 1 MB (uncompressed) over a build-output size check, because
the check also runs after each deploy against the live site.

## Consequences

- `/` and `/journey` load a 587 kB (182 kB gzip) script. `/travel` loads a
  further 1.8 MB (516 kB gzip) chunk.
- The textures are copies. They are regenerated by hand if the `three-globe`
  textures change, which is unlikely.
- Third-party scripts (Firebase) fall outside the budget.
